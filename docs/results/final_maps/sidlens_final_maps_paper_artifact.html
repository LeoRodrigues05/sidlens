What this page is

The paper's map results (RQ1, the introduction's first finding, the abstract, the discussion's design table and the setup) were computed on the old frozen Industrial maps. The final run trains on the v3 maps: 27 per catalog for Industrial and Office, with ZCR twins. This page lists every map result the paper displays, with its value on the old maps, recomputed from the paper's own data package and the old collision-cause run, and on the v3 maps of both catalogs. What the RQ1 text should say

In the order of the draft's paragraphs; each item names where it goes. Draft wording follows docs/paper/WRITING.md.

  • Three digits sort items into categories (RQ1 ¶1; intro finding 1; Figure map-digits c). Share of items in their group's main category among items that share their first three digits with another item, RQ-KMeans / RQ-VAE / MQ, 4×128: Industrial (level-1 categories) 97.0 % / 98.6 % / 79.2 %; null 38–50 %; Office (level-2 categories) 95.3 % / 96.7 % / 79.8 %; null 35–46 %; old maps 97.4 % / 99.4 % / 81.5 %; null 39–50 %. The introduction's "81–99 %" becomes "79–99 %".
  • Residual digits refine their prefix; MQ digits repeat each other (RQ1 ¶2; Figure map-digits b). Variance explained by digit 1 alone, RQ-KMeans / RQ-VAE, 4×128: Industrial 52.8 % / 29.2 %; Office 52.7 % / 41.5 %; old 52.8 % / 34.5 %. Later residual digits alone: 9–20 % (Industrial), 3–19 % (Office). Each MQ digit alone: 38 % (Industrial), 35–38 % (Office).
  • Add for RQ-VAE: given digit 1, digit 2 explains the most variance. Variance explained by digit 2 given digit 1 (digit 1 in brackets), 9 RQ-VAE maps: Industrial 43–59 % (25–37 %); digit 2 > digit 1 in 9/9; Office 38–46 % (34–46 %); digit 2 > digit 1 in 5/9; old 43–59 % (20–42 %); digit 2 > digit 1 in 9/9. This is not new in v3: the draft reports digits alone only, which hides it.
  • The quantizers place item identity in different digits (RQ1 ¶3; plan P4–P5). Share of RQ-VAE's first codebook in use, 9 maps: Industrial 3–45 %; 9/9 at ≤ 50 %; Office 4–100 %; 7/9 at ≤ 50 %; old 2–81 %; 8/9 at ≤ 31 %. RQ-VAE 4×128 items with their own SID after four digits: 99.2 % (Industrial), 99.3 % (Office). RQ-VAE 4×128 items whose full SID any three of their four digits determine: 89.98 % (Industrial), 87.03 % (Office), old 92.54 %. Items in a shared SID at 3×128, RQ-KMeans / MQ: 44.2 % / 55.9 % (Industrial), 57.7 % / 69.6 % (Office).
  • Near-duplicate products set the floor (RQ1 ¶4; table row All). Identical embeddings: 22 items in 11 groups (0.71 %) on Industrial, 117 items in 57 groups (0.66 %) on Office; all 27 maps of each catalog give every identical group one SID. Collided items with a same-brand, near-identical-title bucket-mate in the 5-digit RQ-KMeans maps: Industrial 5×128 71 %, 5×256 79 %, 5×512 94 %; Office 5×128 68 %, 5×256 76 %, 5×512 84 %.
  • Post-processing sets RQ-VAE's rate; now shown directly (RQ1 ¶5; plan P6). Items in a shared SID under RQ-VAE's own nearest codes → in the published table: 5.5–19.8 % → 0.71–2.54 % (Industrial), 7.3–29.9 % → 0.66–1.37 % (Office). Re-running the reassignment from the nearest codes gives back the published table: 9/9 tables item for item (min 100.00 %) on Industrial, 6/9 tables item for item (min 99.99 %) on Office. The same step applied to RQ-KMeans: 6.3–44.2 % → 0.71–0.84 % on Industrial, changing up to 44 % of last digits; 12.6–57.7 % → 0.66–3.21 % on Office. What the loop leaves in RQ-VAE: 5×512: 57 items mapped to one latent point (every unit of the encoder's first hidden layer is zero for them) (Industrial); 26 buckets of 3–5 items in 9 maps, with close but distinct latents (Office). The old analysis could only infer such a collapse; the checkpoints now show it.
  • RQ-KMeans hub codes (RQ1 ¶5). At levels 2–5, the code whose centroid is nearest the origin is the most used at 12/12 levels on Industrial and 10/12 on Office, and holds 9–21 % and 3–10 % of items.
  • MQ digits repeat each other (RQ1 ¶5). Mean pairwise AMI between digits, per map: MQ 0.29–0.33; residual 0.06–0.13 (Industrial), MQ 0.29–0.35; residual 0.04–0.15 (Office). MQ 3×128, observed against independent digits: 55.9 % vs 6.8 % (Industrial), 69.6 % vs 14.2 % (Office). Cause: MQ's encoders never train (every encoder parameter of all 18 MQ models is at its initialisation), so each digit quantizes a different random projection of the same item.
  • ZCR (RQ1 ¶6). Last digits ZCR changes in RQ-KMeans 3×128: 896 (29 %); +5.1 % squared distance on Industrial; 7260 (41 %); +5.2 % squared distance on Office; old 910 (29 %).
  • Implication (RQ1 last paragraph). Unchanged. Add that the reassignment does not reach the identical-input floor on Office maps whose prefix groups are larger than the last codebook.

Other places in the paper

  • Abstract ("depends mostly on post-processing and near-duplicate products"): holds in both catalogs (statements 5 and 6 above).
  • Introduction, finding 1 ("share a product category in 81–99 % of cases"): 79–99 % on v3 (statement 1). "Collision rates depend mostly on post-processing": holds; "most collisions that remain in long RQ-KMeans SIDs are between near-duplicate products": holds.
  • Discussion, design table, row Quantizer: holds with the numbers above. Row Post-processing: drop "Sinkhorn balancing RQ-KMeans 5×512's"; v3 has no balanced table.
  • Discussion, limitation "Map statistics use the input embedding, because RQ-VAE's latent space was not saved": no longer true. The v3 RQ-VAE checkpoints are published; in its own latent, RQ-VAE's digit 1 explains 71–84 % (Industrial), 68–76 % (Office) of the variance (input embedding: 25–37 % (Industrial), 34–46 % (Office)). Keep the input-space statistics for comparability and say the latent ones are in the appendix or supplement.
  • Setup, SID maps (\pending{check on the v3 tables}): RQ-KMeans 3/4/5 are nested at every width in both catalogs. RQ-VAE and MQ depths are separate fits.
  • Setup, ZCR twins (\pending{list of ZCR twins}): 21 on Industrial (MQ_3×128, MQ_3×256, MQ_3×512, MQ_4×128, MQ_4×256, MQ_4×512, MQ_5×128, MQ_5×256, MQ_5×512, rqkmeans_3×128, rqkmeans_3×256, rqkmeans_3×512, rqkmeans_4×128, rqkmeans_4×256, rqkmeans_4×512, rqkmeans_5×128, rqkmeans_5×256, rqkmeans_5×512, rqvae_3×256, rqvae_4×256, rqvae_5×256) and 18 on Office (MQ_3×256, MQ_3×512, MQ_4×256, MQ_4×512, MQ_5×256, MQ_5×512, rqkmeans_3×128, rqkmeans_3×256, rqkmeans_3×512, rqkmeans_4×128, rqkmeans_4×256, rqkmeans_4×512, rqkmeans_5×128, rqkmeans_5×256, rqkmeans_5×512, rqvae_3×256, rqvae_4×256, rqvae_5×256). Office MQ 3×128, 4×128 and 5×128 are infeasible.
  • Setup, data (\pending{Office metadata}): Office labels are built (100 % join). Its level-1 breadcrumb has 3 classes, so Office purity uses level-2 categories (42 classes); say so in the setup.
  • Appendix, "Redundant MQ digits": measure with AMI, not normalized mutual information (claim mq_nmi).
  • Paper plan P5: "92.5 % of RQ-VAE 4×128 items have a unique 3-digit prefix" misdescribes the number. It is the share of items whose full SID is determined by any three of their four digits; the unique-3-digit-prefix share is 79.6 % on the old map and 77.1 % on v3.

Old maps against v3 (Industrial)

  • RQ-KMeans is the same procedure on other hardware. v3 keeps the old first digit for 100 % (8 tables; the old 5×512 was a separate balanced fit: 0 %) of items and the old second digit for W=128 100 % / W=256 89 % / W=512 78 %; later digits differ because FAISS's k-means gives different results on different hardware from level 3 on. RQ-KMeans collision shares move by 1.2 points at most, except 5×512.
  • RQ-KMeans 5×512 was a separate, balanced fit; on v3 it is the nested 5-digit extension of 3×512 and 4×512 (0.64 %; separate fit; identical pairs split 11 → 6.31 %; nested; identical pairs split 0).
  • RQ-VAE and MQ are new fits (RQ-KMeans 3×128 same full SID 33 %; RQ-VAE 4×128 first-digit ARI 0.40; MQ first-digit ARI 0.07–0.12). MQ is also a new implementation (TokenRec's code). Their map-level statistics are close to the old ones (panels b–d), so the statements carry over, but results tied to particular old items or SIDs do not.

Not yet available for the paper

  • Plan 5.3 (how collisions change the reported score): P9–P12, and final plan M3/M4 need the final run's predictions (SID- against item-level Hit@10 per run, native minus ZCR twin, hits by target class). This analysis has none: $SIDLENS_WORK/runs/ holds no exported final-run run yet, and the codebook dataset holds only maps.
  • What the maps already give 5.3 (exploratory): the share of next-item test targets whose SID a different history item carries is 4.6–10.8 % under the RQ-KMeans maps on Industrial and 1.9–5.3 % on Office; repeats are 5.0 % and 5.6 %; the ZCR twins have no SID partners. Copying a history item's full SID can earn a SID-level hit only on these targets and on repeats.
  • Seeds. Each map is one fit; there is no fit-to-fit interval for any map statistic.
