Structure exp4 · run /nfs-stor/leo.rodrigues/sidlens_work/derived/structure/exp4_final_maps/327442 · both catalogs, 54 native maps and their ZCR twins · no recommender is run

Summary

  • The tables are what they claim to be. All 54 native tables and 39 ZCR twins match their recorded hashes; every ZCR twin keeps its native prefixes, has no shared SID and changes the minimum number of last digits. RQ-KMeans maps of one width are truncations of one fit in both catalogs, which the draft marked as unchecked. The RQ-VAE and MQ weights reproduce their tables.
  • Residual digits refine their prefix; MQ digits repeat each other. In RQ-KMeans 4×128, digits 1–4 on their own explain 53, 16, 15, 12 % of the embedding variance (Industrial; Office 53, 19, 13, 11 %). Every MQ digit explains about the same share (largest/smallest 1.01–1.11 on Industrial, 1.02–1.09 on Office).
  • In RQ-VAE, digit 2 carries the most meaning, as it did in the old maps. On Industrial it uses 3–45 % (9/9 at <= 50 %) of its first codebook, and given digit 1, digit 2 explains 43–59 % of the variance against 25–37 % for digit 1 (Office 38–46 % against 34–46 %). The old maps show the same (paper page); the draft reports digits alone only. Digits 4–5 add at most 1.5 %: they separate items.
  • Three digits sort items into categories. Among items that share their first three digits with another item (4×128), the share in their group's main category is, on Industrial (level-1 categories): RQ-KMeans 97.0 % (null 44.8 %; 43 % of items still grouped); MQ 79.2 % (null 37.9 %; 55 % of items still grouped). On Office (level-2 categories): RQ-KMeans 95.3 % (null 40.8 %; 56 % of items still grouped); MQ 79.8 % (null 35.2 %; 69 % of items still grouped).
  • RQ-VAE's low collision rate is its post-processing, now shown directly. Its own nearest codes collide 5.5–19.8 % of items (Office 7.3–29.9 %); the published tables collide 0.71–2.54 % (0.66–1.37 %). Re-running the reassignment loop from the nearest codes reproduces 9/9 tables item for item (min 100.00 %) on Industrial and 6/9 tables item for item (min 99.99 %) on Office.
  • Near-duplicate products are the floor. In the 5-digit RQ-KMeans maps, 5×128 71 %, 5×256 79 %, 5×512 94 % of collided items share their SID with a same-brand, near-identical-title neighbour (Office 5×128 68 %, 5×256 76 %, 5×512 84 %).
  • MQ's dependent digits have a simple cause. MQ's encoders never train: every encoder weight in all 18 MQ models is still at its random initialisation, so each digit quantizes a different random projection of the same item. Mean pairwise AMI between digits is 0.29–0.33 for MQ against 0.06–0.11 for RQ-KMeans (Industrial).
  • Many test targets share their SID with a different history item (exploratory). RQ-KMeans maps: 4.6–10.8 % of Industrial next-item targets, 1.9–5.3 % of Office's; repeats are 5.0 % / 5.6 %. ZCR twins have none.

Validation (Part 0)

  • Hashes: every native table matches the sha256 in done.json and tables.json; every ZCR index matches its manifest.json and names its native table by hash.
  • Nesting: the 3- and 4-digit RQ-KMeans tables are the prefixes of the 5-digit table for every item at every width, in both catalogs. RQ-VAE and MQ depths are separate fits.
  • RQ-VAE: the checkpoint's encoder and codebooks give every table's digits 1 to D−1 for every item, and the exported last-level residuals to within 1.5e-5.
  • MQ: the eval-mode encoders give every digit of every table, except one Office item in MQ 5×512 whose two nearest codewords differ in distance by a relative 1.8e-7 (float32 tie).
  • RQ-KMeans: the tables cannot be reproduced by refitting on this cluster. On Industrial 3×128, per-level k-means and faiss's ResidualQuantizer give digits 1–2 exactly and digit 3 for about a third of items, at 1, 8 and 96 threads (amendment A1). The published codebooks reproduce every table by nearest centroid, so the post-processing and hub-code analyses use them; Part C is a counterfactual on this cluster's refits.
