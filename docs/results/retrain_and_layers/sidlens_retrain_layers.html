Retrained recommenders and what each layer holds

Industrial_and_Scientific, Qwen2.5-1.5B next-item recommenders. Test split (3,681 events, 1,606 users) unless stated. Intervals are 95% paired user-bootstrap intervals (2,000 draws); they exclude training-seed variance.

recap · 28 Sep – 4 Oct

Last week: copying helps mostly where the history already holds the answer

The week started from three AR results of 27 Sep. Replacing the most recent history item costs the golden first digit 0.57 / 0.51 nats (RQ-KMeans 3×128 / RQ-VAE 4×128). A history item that already matches the target's first d digits is copied at digit d (1.35 / 1.06 nats). A single attention edge in layers 14–27 carries that copy (0.40 / 0.36 nats). Last week asked whether copying helps real recommendations, how it is computed, and what in the data it exploits. Pairs of numbers below are RQ-KMeans 3×128 / RQ-VAE 4×128.

Does copying help the recommendations?

28 Sep
Copy block inside beam searchcontrolled exp6

Blocking copy reads in layers 14–27 lowers exact-SID HR@10 by 5.7 / 3.3 points: 27.3 / 10.1 when the true next item's SID is already in the history, and 2.1 / 3.0 otherwise. A matched control block moves it by −0.03 / −0.11. With plain beam search the drop is 4.0 / 3.2 points.

28 Sep
Audit of the archived AR decoder

The archived beams sampled (temperature 0.7, top-k 20, top-p 0.8, repetition penalty 1.1), and 18.9% / 15.0% of list entries were not catalogue SIDs. The surviving RQ-VAE model reproduces none of the 3,681 archived RQ-VAE lists, so it is not the sweep's model.

3 Oct
Collision credit in copyingretro exp7

Scored per item, the copy benefit shrinks. For RQ-KMeans 3×128, targets that share a history item's SID but are a different item (11% of rows) supply 56% of the HR@10 loss, and only 28% of the HR@1 benefit survives. For RQ-VAE, near-duplicate variants supply 80% of the loss. On truly new targets the item-level change is −0.2 / −0.1 points.

4 Oct
Copied reviewsexploratory, no protocol

About 15% of targets are one review that Amazon copied onto a variant item already in the history. They supply 33 / 42% of SID HR@10 hits. Without them and exact duplicates, HR@10 falls from 24.0% to 16.4% and from 14.4% to 7.2%.

How copying is computed

28 Sep
Logit lens and probesrepresentation exp1

The final first digit equals the most recent item's first digit in 64% / 53% of rows. The copy preference appears at layers 20–23 (RQ-KMeans) and 11 and 20 (RQ-VAE). A linear probe never reads the true next item's first digit better than the rule “use the most recent item's first digit”.

28 Sep
DiffGRM history use and copy routecontrolled exp7, exp8

DiffGRM behaves like the AR model: replacing the most recent item costs 0.56 / 0.48 nats, and prefix-matched copying 0.35 / 0.96. The copy runs through the encoder. The decoder's direct read of the item carries nothing alone. Blocking both routes removes about two thirds of the effect.

30 Sep
Sparse autoencoders on AR residualsrepresentation exp2

Copy features at the first-digit readout are causal from layer 20: ablating them costs 0.10 nats at L20 and 0.21 / 0.36 at L24. Sharp prefix-match features (AUC up to 0.97) have no causal effect at the readout. No target content beyond the history.

30 Sep
Order vs timecontrolled exp9

RQ-KMeans follows list position, not item or time: swapping the two most recent items while keeping their dates flips 50% of first-digit decisions and 42% of top-1 SIDs, with HR@10 unchanged. RQ-VAE is only weakly positional.

3 Oct
Last digit of a prefix-matching itemcontrolled exp10

RQ-KMeans copies the matching item's last digit, which costs sibling targets. RQ-VAE does the opposite in sibling rows: blocking the edge raises top-1 = that item by about 12 points and lowers top-1 = the sibling target by about 10. These are secondary measures: RQ-VAE's declared primary (pooled, control-subtracted) points the same way, but its interval includes 0.

What the data and maps supply

30 Sep
Time structureretro exp6

In this data “recent” means the same day: once an item's age is known, its position adds almost nothing. Same-day rows carry 87 / 88% of exp6's copy benefit. Duplicate review records are 2.7% of test rows but 11 / 17% of HR@10 hits.

3 Oct
Why items share a SIDstructure exp3

Each quantizer family collides for a different reason. RQ-VAE's near-zero rate comes from upstream's last-digit de-duplication loop. Near-duplicate variants set the floor. Shallow RQ-KMeans collisions come from a near-origin hub code, and MQ digits are redundant. 44.8% of items share a SID at RQ-KMeans 3×128.

This week's results, strongest first

The layer-map runs finished on Sunday 4 Oct; their analysis and everything else below is from 5–6 Oct. Each result says whether it is ready to show.

    show · strongest new resultrepresentation / exp3 · information map

    Repeat recommendations settle around layer 17, others around 23

    For the two original checkpoints we let each model write its own recommendation for every test row and recorded every layer while it did. On these recordings we trained readers (a tuned lens, whole-SID decoders and sparse autoencoders) and asked what each layer holds. All of it is observational: readable does not mean used.

    • Planning ahead is not shown. The recommendation decoder already scores 62% / 49% at layer 0, after one attention pass over the history. The decoder works out much of the recommendation from history information itself, so a high score at layer 26 does not show that the model has planned later digits at the answer start.
    • Better than the rule is not the same as beyond the history. A decoder can beat “recommend the most recent item” by, for example, recommending the item before it. The SAE study (representation exp2) found no information about the true next item beyond what the history gives.
    • Greedy recommendations. The recommendation here is the model's greedy answer. It equals the true next item in of rows and the plain beam search's top-1 in .
    show · methods resultsubstrate / exp1 · acceptance

    12 of 18 retrained models match the deleted ones

    The August sweep deleted the weights of 26 of its 28 AR runs after scoring them. We retrained the 18 next-item models of the width-128 and width-512 grid with the recorded recipe and compared each with the deleted model's archived ranked lists on the same test rows. A model is accepted when Hit@10 moves by at most 1.5 points and NDCG@10 by at most 1.0 point. Only accepted models enter any later result.

    not ready to presentsubstrate / exp1 · arm B

    Collision-free maps score lower at the SID level

    ZCR rewrites five RQ-KMeans maps so that no two items share a SID. Each ZCR model was trained with the same recipe as its native twin. These are reference scores only; no protocol covers the comparison yet.

    Do not read the gap as “ZCR makes the model worse”. In a native map several items can share one SID, and SID-level scoring counts any of them as a hit. In retro exp7, 42% of native RQ-KMeans 3×128 hits came from such shared SIDs. A fair comparison needs item-level scoring under its own protocol.
